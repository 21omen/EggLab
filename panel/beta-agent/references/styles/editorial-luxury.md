# Style card — Editorial luxury (fashion / fragrance / spirits)

**Use for:** perfume, jewellery, spirits, fashion, skincare, hotels — anything that must look expensive.
**Say it as:** "shoot it like a fragrance campaign."

## Palette
- Near-monochrome: black `#0A0A0A`, bone `#EDE8E0`, and at most one restrained
  accent — champagne `#C9A96A` or oxblood `#6E1F24`.
- Most of the canvas is one of the two neutrals. Contrast comes from light, not
  from colour.

## Type
- A high-contrast serif (Didone) for the name, set with very wide letter-spacing,
  often all-caps and small in a large field.
- A tiny grotesque caps for legal, size and detail lines. Two families, extreme
  size difference.

## Image treatment
- Studio light with a hard falloff: one key, deep shadow, specular highlight on
  the material (glass, metal, skin).
- Black-and-white or heavily desaturated; a duotone into the accent is acceptable.
  Never a busy background — a sweep or a wall with gradient light.

## Ornament vocabulary
A hairline keyline box · a thin rule under the name · a small caps ingredient or
volume line · an embossed-looking seal · a page-number style index mark ·
generous, obviously expensive margins.

## Print artifacts (finish pass)
Very fine grain (luxury must not look plastic) · a soft vignette · subtle paper
texture · no halftone, no misregistration — this style wants control.

## Composition
One object, centred *or* on a third, surrounded by deliberate emptiness. Type
small and far from the subject; the space between them is the luxury signal.

## Avoid
Saturated colour · clutter · badges · loud accents · drop shadows · grainy
"grunge" textures · more than one accent moment.

## World-line seed
"Shot on a single 1m softbox at 1/8 power, retouched to keep the dust off the
glass, printed on 300gsm uncoated bone stock."
