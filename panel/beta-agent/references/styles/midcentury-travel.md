# Style card — Mid-century travel poster (1950s–60s)

**Use for:** destinations, hospitality, coffee and food, outdoors, anything warm and optimistic.
**Say it as:** "paint it like a 1950s travel poster."

## Palette
- Sun-faded and limited: turquoise `#2E9AA6`, burnt orange `#E06B32`, mustard
  `#E8B23A`, deep green `#2F6B4F`, cream `#F3E9D2`.
- Four inks max, flat, with one clearly dominant.

## Type
- One hand-lettered or humanist display face for the destination name — chunky,
  slightly irregular, sometimes with a swash.
- Supporting line in tracked-out caps; a tiny block of detail text at the bottom.

## Image treatment
- **Illustrated, not photographic** — flat shapes with one or two tones per
  object, simplified geometry, airbrush-free.
- Landscape built from stacked planes: foreground silhouette, mid-ground subject,
  distant band of colour. Grain and a light halftone unify the planes.

## Ornament vocabulary
A sun disc or starburst rays · a route line with stops · a luggage or baggage
tag · a dashed border · a circular "via" stamp · an airline/line logotype in a
bar · a compass rose · altitude or distance ticks.

## Print artifacts (finish pass)
Paper grain and slight yellowing · a whisper of halftone · soft edge wear at the
border · one element very slightly off-register.

## Composition
Big subject low or off-centre, sky occupying a large calm band for the title,
strong horizon rule. Depth from overlapping planes, not from shadows.

## Avoid
Photorealism · gradients in the sky · drop shadows · glossy highlights · more
than four colours · cluttered detail (poster illustration simplifies).

## World-line seed
"Offset-printed in Chicago on soft uncoated stock, colours faded by sixty years
of window light."
