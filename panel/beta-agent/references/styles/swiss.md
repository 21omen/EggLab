# Style card — Swiss International (1950s–70s)

**Use for:** architecture, galleries, tech, reports, premium minimal brands, museums.
**Say it as:** "set it on a Swiss grid."

## Palette
- Usually one colour plus black and the paper: white `#FFFFFF`, black `#000000`,
  and a single signal red `#E30613` or blue `#0055A4`.
- Colour is structural — a red bar or a blue field carries meaning, it is not
  decoration.

## Type
- One grotesque, one weight family: Helvetica, Akzidenz-Grotesk, Univers.
- Flush-left, ragged right, never justified, never centred. Extreme size contrast
  — a huge headline against 9pt body, nothing between.
- Tight leading on the big type, generous leading on the small.

## Image treatment
- Photography is documentary and **full but cold** — or one hard geometric crop.
  Duotone acceptable; heavy texture is not.
- Images snap to the grid: full-bleed columns, half-page blocks, no floating.

## Ornament vocabulary
Grid rules you can see · a thin hairline separating columns · page or figure
numbers set large · a solid colour bar as a section marker · a single geometric
mark (circle, triangle) · captions in small caps under every image.

## Print artifacts (finish pass)
Almost none — Swiss work is clean by design. Use a whisper of grain only so the
flat colour does not look like a screenshot, and keep edges razor sharp.

## Composition
Asymmetric grid, 6–12 columns, everything aligned to it. Negative space is
active: an empty third of the canvas is a decision, and the single red element
sits in it.

## Avoid
Ornament for its own sake · textures · drop shadows · rounded corners · centred
type · more than one typeface · the accent used more than twice.

## World-line seed
"Printed in Zürich on coated stock, photographed flat under even north light."
