# Visual principles

These are decision principles, not a rigid style template.

## Creative objective
- Reduce the brief to one clear idea that can be understood quickly.
- Design for the actual audience, channel, context, culture and viewing distance.
- Distinguish the communication hierarchy from decorative hierarchy.
- Make the focal point deliberate; every other element supports, explains or frames it.
- Prefer a specific visual concept over a generic category cliché.

## Sequence
1. Confirm output format, dimensions, crop and safe zones.
2. Establish a clear concept and focal point.
3. Choose composition, camera/viewpoint, palette and light.
4. Establish type hierarchy and copy placement.
5. Determine which elements need generated pixels and which should remain editable.
6. Add depth, supporting detail and finishing only when they strengthen the idea.

## Composition and legibility tests
- Squint test: the focal point and major hierarchy should survive reduced detail.
- Thumbnail test: inspect around 10% size.
- Two-second test: a viewer should quickly understand the primary message.
- Edge test: inspect all four frame edges for accidental tangencies and crop errors.
- Contrast test: check the smallest meaningful text and important subject boundaries.
- Viewing-context test: verify at actual device/display/print scale, not only zoomed in.

## Hierarchy
Use size, weight, contrast, position, negative space, alignment, color and repetition as related tools. Do not maximize all of them simultaneously. Establish primary, secondary and tertiary levels. Use intentional asymmetry where appropriate, but preserve balance and clear reading order.

## Grid and spacing
- Choose a grid appropriate to the format and content density.
- Keep important text and logos within a deliberate safe area.
- Use consistent spacing increments, then apply optical corrections where geometry alone looks wrong.
- Avoid near-tangencies between type, subject and frame edges.
- Preserve enough negative space for the focal content to read.

## Palette and lighting
- Start with a restrained palette; a 60/30/10 split is a useful starting heuristic, not a law.
- Preserve brand and product colors where they are fixed requirements.
- Make light direction coherent across subjects, props and environment.
- Shadows, highlights, reflections, haze and depth cues should share a plausible physical logic.

## Avoiding clichés
Do not default to familiar visual tropes merely because they match a keyword. Find a more specific metaphor, object relationship, camera treatment, material treatment or composition. Use references to learn structure and craft, not to copy protected expressive details or logos.

## Design choices should be explained briefly
When presenting a direction, state the central idea, focal hierarchy, palette/light logic and one important trade-off. Avoid narrating every trivial design decision.
