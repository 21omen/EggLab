# Style card — Risograph flyer

**Use for:** events, zines, indie gigs, markets, cafés, anything handmade and cheap-in-a-good-way.
**Say it as:** "run it like a risograph flyer."

## Palette — two inks, plus the paper
- Two spot colours maximum, chosen to overprint: fluorescent pink `#FF48B0`,
  riso blue `#0078BF`, green `#00A95C`, yellow `#FFE800`.
- Paper shows through: warm white `#F7F3E8`, and **leave real unprinted areas** —
  riso ink coverage is the whole aesthetic.
- Where the two inks overlap you get a third colour — plan for it deliberately.

## Type
- One bold condensed or a chunky grotesque for the headline; a plain mono or
  typewriter face for details. Two families is the ceiling.
- Headline often all-caps, slightly too big for the box it sits in, allowed to
  wrap awkwardly. Imperfection reads as authenticity.

## Image treatment
- Halftone, coarse and visible, in a single ink. Photos are screened hard —
  dots you can count at 100%.
- Misregistration is a feature: 2–6 px offset between layers.

## Ornament vocabulary
Hand-drawn arrows · stars and asterisks · dotted cut lines · a boxed "FREE" or
price tag · torn or wavy edges · a repeating stamp pattern in the margin ·
`№` and date marks.

## Print artifacts (finish pass)
Heavy grain · dust and scratches · visible halftone · deliberate misregistration
· slightly uneven ink density across the sheet.

## Composition
Dense and stacked — flyers earn their energy from crowding. Two or three
hierarchy levels crammed together, everything flush-left or rotated a few degrees,
at least one element bleeding off the edge.

## Avoid
Soft shadows · glossy gradients · photorealistic 3D · anything that looks
airbrushed · four colours.

## World-line seed
"Photocopied twice, pulled off the drum while still warm, stapled to a
lamppost in the rain."
