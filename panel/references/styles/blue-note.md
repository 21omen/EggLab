# Style card — Blue Note (1950s–60s jazz sleeve)

**Use for:** jazz, late-night, spirits, audio gear, anything wanting "cool, intelligent, expensive".
**Say it as:** "printing it like a 1960s Blue Note sleeve."

## Palette — three inks, one rationed
- Ground: cream paper `#EFE7D6` or deep midnight blue `#14213A`.
- Primary ink: black `#111111` or paper white on the dark ground.
- **Accent: exactly one, used once or twice only** — burnt orange `#D2541F`,
  hot teal `#1D6E6B`, or mustard `#C9971F`. Treat it like expensive ink: if it
  appears three times it is too many.

## Type
- One grotesque, heavy and tight — Helvetica/Neue Haas in spirit, condensed
  variants for the title. No serif, no script, no second family.
- Title set **enormous**, often split across two lines with a deliberate break,
  letters nearly touching. Type is an image, not a label.
- Credits set micro-tight in a block: `SIDE A · 33⅓ RPM · STEREO` style — small,
  tracked-out caps, stacked.

## Image treatment
- Photography is **never full colour**. Duotone or tinted into the palette, high
  contrast, crushed blacks, a visible halftone screen.
- Crop hard and asymmetric — the subject is cut by the frame, off-centre, often
  occupying one half or one diagonal.

## Ornament vocabulary (your support elements)
Catalogue number · `SIDE A` / `33⅓ RPM` marks · a single hairline rule under the
credit block · a small square or bar of the accent ink · registration tick marks ·
the record label name in a keyline box.

## Print artifacts (finish pass)
Film grain over everything · halftone dots on the photo block · slight ink
misregistration on one element · dust and paper fibre · no gloss, no glow.

## Composition
Structural negative space paired with one big graphic move: a hard colour panel
that breaks at a rule, title straddling the break, photo bleeding off one edge.
Asymmetric, flush-left, never centred.

## Avoid
Glowing type · gradients · sparkles · chrome · the accent used as a fill for
large areas · centring everything · a saxophone silhouette (that is the cliché,
not the style).

## World-line seed
"Shot on a rented 4×5 in a Harlem studio at midnight, printed offset on uncoated
cream stock with two inks and one rationed accent."
