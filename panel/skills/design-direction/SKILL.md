# Design Direction

Activate for new design, poster, advertisement, packaging, campaign artwork, brand-led composition or subjective design review.

1. State the job in one sentence: audience, medium/placement and desired action/feeling.
2. Separate fixed content from open decisions; preserve exact strings and supplied facts.
3. Consider at least two concepts internally; reject stock category imagery when a stronger idea exists.
4. Select one concept and express it in a short phrase. One dominant visual idea per piece.
5. Set format/safe zones/grid, then composition, focal subject, palette, typography and finishing treatment—in that order.
6. Choose a visual profile before loading a style card. Load one matching card only when useful; do not force a random card.
7. Design for target viewing size; check thumbnail hierarchy and two-second comprehension.
8. Each support element must clarify, identify, structure or create deliberate rhythm.
9. Hand off direction to asset planning and PhotoCraft as needed.

**Heuristics, not laws:** one dominant focal point; usually no more than two type families; generous margins unless a bleed is intentional; coherent light/material behavior. A single-object ad may use a large hero, but place/mood work may prioritize space. Photography is a strong default for physical realism; illustration is right when its language is the idea.

Load `references/design/visual-principles.md` for layout; `references/design/finishing-and-support.md` for finish/minimalism; one matching card from `references/styles/` only after style selection.

Record objective, audience, placement, concept, profile, composition, palette, type direction, style card, must-keep and must-avoid in job state.
